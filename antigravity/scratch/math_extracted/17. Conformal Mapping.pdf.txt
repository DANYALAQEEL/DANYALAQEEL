Introduction
Conformal mappings are invaluable to the engineers and physicists 
as an aid in solving problems in potential theory. They are a standard 
method for solving boundary value problems in two-dimensional 
potential theory and yield rich applications in electrostatics, heat 
flow, and fluid flow.
The main feature of conformal mappings is that they are angle-
preserving (except at some critical points) and allow a geometric 
approach to solve boundary value problems in Complex Analysis.

Conformal Mapping
A non-constant linear mapping acts by rotating, magnifying, and 
translating points in the complex plane.
As a result, the angle between any two intersecting arcs in the 
𝑧−plane is equal to the angle between the images of the arcs in the 
𝑤−plane under a linear mapping.
Complex mappings that have this angle-preserving property are called 
conformal mappings.

Conformal Behavior of Analytic Functions
A nice property of a complex analytic function 𝑓(𝑧) = 𝑢+ 𝑖𝑣, is 
that it preserves angles at each point where 𝑓′ 𝑧≠0, i.e., the 
angle between any two domain curves is the same as the angle 
between image curves, except at critical points, that is, points at 
which the derivative 𝑓′ is zero.

Theorem
If f is an analytic function in a domain 𝐷containing 𝑧0, and if
𝑓′(𝑧0) ≠0,
then the mapping 𝑤= 𝑓(𝑧) is a conformal
mapping at 𝑧0.
